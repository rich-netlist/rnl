RNL Viewer 0.5.0 works on phones and tablets, applies the display settings a manifest proposes, and takes its languages from the project's list. It goes with RNL core 0.5.0, in which a role can take every kind of object of 9.4.

## Phones and tablets

- **Touch on the drawing.** One finger drags the view; two zoom it around their middle as they spread or pinch, and drag it as their middle moves. A tap does what a click does, and a double tap what a double click does: it opens a subcircuit instance, or fits the drawing. Gestures on the drawing neither scroll nor zoom the page. The mouse, the wheel and the keys work as before, also on a laptop with a touch screen.
- **A phone shows two pages**, the schematic and the side panel, each the whole screen, upright or lying down; the bar at the bottom switches them, and each stays as it was left: the view, the level, the selection, the side panel's view and where its text is scrolled. With an object selected, **View in Netlist** in the status bar shows its line in the netlist, and **View in Drawing** brings it into the middle of the drawing. Opening a file, going into or out of a subcircuit, and picking a level in the hierarchy show the schematic. The netlist's options are behind an **Options** button. Lying down, the status bar and the switch bar share one row and the problems start folded.
- **A tablet held upright** has the side panel under the drawing, as wide as the screen; its handle drags it taller or lower, and the height is kept. Hide the Side Panel folds it to its tabs; a tap on a tab unfolds it. Lying down, the side panel is beside the drawing, as on a desktop.
- **On a touch screen** the menu titles, the tabs and the side panel's buttons are big enough for a finger, the tabs scroll sideways when they do not fit, menus stay on the screen, and nothing flashes when it is tapped. The status bar says a double tap opens a subcircuit instance.
- On a phone and in a tablet's bottom panel, the problems are as tall as what they list, so a few leave the netlist the rest of the room.
- A narrow browser or desktop window shows the two pages too, and one taller than it is wide the side panel under the drawing; other windows keep their layout.

## Display settings from a manifest

- A manifest can propose display settings: the view of its level, the background, the colour scheme, whether usages and current directions are shown, and the grid. When an external manifest (`?manifest=`), a project or a manifest of links proposes some, the viewer lists them and applies those you keep (**Apply Selected**, or **Don't Apply**). A setting the viewer does not know is named and left out.
- The settings kept hold while a file of the manifest is shown: a file of another goes back to your own settings, and a file of the manifest again brings them back, unasked. They never replace your saved settings.
- **Save Project**, **Download Project** and **Download Manifest** ask whether to also write the current view, background, colour scheme, usages, current directions and grid into what they write.

## Languages

- **View › Language** lists English and each language that the project offers for the viewers (`i18n/languages.json`), by its name in itself: English and 简体中文, as before. A text not yet translated shows in English.
- If the web viewer cannot load a language's translations, it starts without that language and says so in the status bar, in English; it tries again at the next start.
- **Help › Desktop Viewer** (web) opens the site's page of the tools in the first of the browser's languages that the project offers, else in English.

## Other changes

- Netlists whose definitions give a role the kind `text`, `label`, `annotation` or `bundle` now apply and draw; they used to stop with an error (core 0.5.0).

Windows requires 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 and Microsoft Edge WebView2. The installers download WebView2 when it is missing. Linux requires x86_64, WebKitGTK 4.1 and a glibc version compatible with Ubuntu 22.04 or newer.

The macOS app has an ad-hoc signature only, with no Developer ID and no Apple notarization. If macOS blocks the first start of a download you trust, choose Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security, or run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/RNL Viewer.app"`.

The downloads are not code-signed with a publisher certificate. Windows may display a SmartScreen or unknown-publisher warning. Verify downloads against `SHA256SUMS.txt`, for example with `shasum -a 256` on macOS.
