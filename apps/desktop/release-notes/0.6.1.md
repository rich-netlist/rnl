RNL Viewer 0.6.1 carries the core of RNL core 0.6.1, which is released with it, and reads large designs much faster. It also fixes the check boxes of Solve › Settings on macOS and Linux, the web viewer now routes each net again by default when it solves, and Hide duplicate usages draws one frame where two were drawn for the same devices. From this release on, the local viewer for older enterprise Linux is built and released with every Viewer release. Nothing changes in the files the viewer reads or writes.

## Large designs open faster

- **Large designs are read much faster** in the desktop viewer, the web viewer and the local viewer. Parts of the reading in core 0.6.0 took a time that grew faster than the levels did, most of all in levels of many instances whose pins the standard package's fill gives stubs or labels; in the core 0.6.1 they carry, that time grows with the level. On two designs from a local set, the core reads in about a third and a fifth of the time of 0.6.0, as the desktop viewer does; the web viewer and the local viewer, whose core is WebAssembly, open the first in about half the time or less. The core's notes give the measures.
- What is drawn does not change: the diagnostics, the model and the drawing are those of 0.6.0.

## Solving

- **The check boxes of Solve › Settings show on macOS and Linux again.** In the desktop viewer on macOS and Linux, whose page is drawn by WebKit, the settings that are on or off, such as `reroute_nets` and `join_trees`, had a box of no width, so that they could not be seen or set. They are drawn as on Windows and in the browsers.
- **The web viewer routes each net again by default** (`reroute_nets`) when it solves: each net of three pins or more is routed again, one wire at a time, and a wire routed again is kept where the net then has fewer crossings and bends. A solve takes a little longer for it. Solve › Settings shows the setting checked as its default, and a value you chose yourself is kept. The desktop viewer and `rnl solve` keep the solver's own default. (Web only.)

## Usages

- **Hide duplicate usages compares what is drawn.** Usages whose drawn members are the same are duplicates, however their roles group them: the instances, and the usages with a symbol of their own, such as ports; a pin counts as the device it is the pin of. Points and nets do not count. So a `$MIRRORED_LAYOUT` about an axis point and a `$MATCHED` or a current mirror of the same two transistors now draw one frame, where two were drawn one inside the other. An axis that is an instance, or a port, is drawn, and still counts.
- **Changed from 0.6.0:** two usages of one instance at different points, or with different pins of it, were drawn apart; they are now one set, of which one frame is drawn. Which frame is drawn, and the *duplicate of …* of the others' rows, are as before.

## Translations

- The Japanese text of Help › Keyboard Shortcuts for Edit Text is worded as a sentence, as the other lines of the help are.

## Downloads for older enterprise Linux

- **Every Viewer release now has the local viewer for older enterprise Linux among its downloads**, with the version of the release: one static program, for CentOS 7, RHEL 8 and 9 and any Linux x86_64 of kernel 3.2 or newer, that serves this viewer on the computer and shows it in a browser, and the same with Firefox ESR beside it. It was released on its own before, as local viewer 0.1.0; Viewer 0.6.0 had its packages added after the release.
- Help › Check for Updates is still offered by the desktop viewer only.

### Known trade-offs

- The trade-offs of Viewer 0.6.0 still hold.
- A level of many instances whose pins the standard package's fill gives stubs still reads in a time that grows faster than the level; the core 0.6.1 notes say more.

Windows requires 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 and Microsoft Edge WebView2. The installers download WebView2 when it is missing. Linux requires x86_64, WebKitGTK 4.1 and a glibc version compatible with Ubuntu 22.04 or newer; the local viewer needs neither, only a browser.

The macOS app has an ad-hoc signature only, with no Developer ID and no Apple notarization. If macOS blocks the first start of a download you trust, choose Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security, or run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/RNL Viewer.app"`.

The downloads are not code-signed with a publisher certificate. Windows may display a SmartScreen or unknown-publisher warning. Verify downloads against `SHA256SUMS.txt`, for example with `shasum -a 256` on macOS.
