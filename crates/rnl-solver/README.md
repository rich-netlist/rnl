# rnl-solver

The solver of RNL descriptions. It takes the problem of one drawing level, the `LevelProblem` that the runtime builds when it reads a document with `Options::solver()` (see [For a solver](../rnl-runtime/README.md#for-a-solver)), resolves its geometric facts as 8.5 says, places and orients its objects and routes its ROUTEs (13.7), and gives a `Solution`, which the runtime writes back as the level's record of the solver:

```rust
let solved = rnl_solver::solve(problem, &options);
let hash = rnl_runtime::problem::sig1(problem, &options.canonical());
let written = rnl_runtime::solution::write_record(problem, &solved.solution, rnl_solver::AUTHOR, &hash);
```

`rnl solve FILE -o DIR` of [`rnl-cli`](../rnl-cli/README.md#solve) does this for every drawing level of a document: it reads the files as a solver reads them, solves each level, writes its record back at the end of the level's text, and reads the document again before the next level.

**Status: solver v1, stages S1–S6.** The crate, its options, its exact arithmetic, the resolution of 8.5, the structure of a level, the search, the coordinates, the routing and the layered score are in place: `resolve` keeps or discards each fact, exactly, with all its minimal conflict groups and the hints by statement; `solve` then searches candidates structure first, places each by a lexicographic LP rounded to the grid, every fact kept holding exactly, routes the ROUTEs of the best of them around the symbols, with branch points and junctions, and takes the best by the layered objectives in force on their wires. `rnl solve` writes the results back into the files, and the viewer's tests open files it solved (`tests/viewer/specs/solved.spec.js`, the end-to-end demonstration of S6). [Not yet done](#not-yet-done) lists what solver v1 leaves out.

## Pipeline

```text
LevelProblem
  → resolve (8.5)    fail each fact that has no solution on its own; keep or discard the others in their
                     order, exactly; a witness that satisfies those kept
  → structure        devices, nets, supply and ground, current depth, groups, rows, what is left to decide
  → search           candidates: P0 and a beam search up the tree of groups, as the objectives in force ask
  → coords           each candidate placed by a lexicographic LP, every fact kept holding, rounded to the grid
  → route (13.7)     the best `beam` by the estimate: the ROUTEs routed by a rectilinear A* around the symbols
  → score            the layered objectives measured on the wires; the best is the solution
Solution, SolveReport
```

Each step is a module; the sections below describe them, and [Modules](#modules) lists them. Every decision is exact. Only the resolution of 8.5 is limited in time, as the draft 0.3 allows (8.5, see [Time limit](#time-limit)); everything after it is counted work, never time. Without a time limit, and whenever no check reaches it, the same problem and options give the same solution on every machine.

## Interface

- `resolve(&LevelProblem, &SolverOptions) -> Resolved`: the resolution of 8.5 alone (see [Resolution](#resolution)): `kept`, `discarded`, `failed` and `statements` as in the report below, the `work` done, `errors`, `warnings`, `notes`, and the witness, read with `position(object)`, `orientation(object)` and `point(problem, &PointRef)`.
- `solve(&LevelProblem, &SolverOptions) -> Solved`: the `Solution`, and a `SolveReport` of what became of each fact. 8.5 first checks each fact on its own, then takes the others in the order of `LevelProblem::facts`, which is their rank, and keeps each that fits with those kept before it. `failed` lists the facts that have no solution on their own: each contradicts itself and fails (the draft 0.3, 8.5; 7.6), with an error in `errors`, such as `the fact [3] ALIGN (amp.sp:12) contradicts itself and fails (8.5, 7.6)`; it takes no part in the resolution, neither kept nor discarded, in no group and no hint by statement, and the level is solved as if it were not written. `kept` lists the facts kept; `discarded` each fact discarded, with `groups`, every minimal conflict group it has (the draft 0.3, 8.5): each a set of facts kept before it that has no solution together with it and from which no fact can be left out, sorted by rank inside and between groups (`first_group` gives the first, `in_groups` every fact of them); `complete`, false when the time limit ran out before the groups were all found, so that the hint says that its list is not complete, and for a fact of priority low whose groups were not all sought (`hints_for_low`, below); and `undecided` when its check did not finish within the time limit, its `groups` then empty. `statements` gives the hints by statement (the draft 0.3, 8.5): for each `Statement` whose facts are in a group of a fact whose hints are given, keyed by where it is written (file, line, column) and the chain of usages its facts were expanded from, or by the point for the coordinates of a point, a `StatementHint` with those facts and the facts discarded because of them. `warnings` has one sentence for each fact discarded undecided (7.6), naming the fact by its rank, its label, where its statement is written and the definitions it was expanded from, such as `the fact [12] ALIGN (amp.sp:40, from $MIRROR_PAIR) was not decided within the time limit of 5 s and is discarded (8.5)`. `inert_objectives` lists the objectives that are on but have nothing to work with; `unrouted` the ROUTEs whose nets the router left in parts (`Unrouted`: the ROUTE, the net, the parts, whether its budget ran out), which is not an error, the viewer drawing flight lines for what is left; `work` counts what was done: the nodes and pivots of every resolution, the candidates and the A* expansions.
- `solve_with_clock(&LevelProblem, &SolverOptions, &dyn Clock)` and `resolve_with_clock(…)`: the same, with the time limit measured on the caller's clock. The crate reads no clock (it builds for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`): `Clock::elapsed()` gives the seconds since a start of the clock's own, and the limit of a level runs from the reading when its resolution begins. `solve` and `resolve` measure on `StoppedClock`, which never advances, so the limit is never reached and the result is that of 8.5 whatever the options say; `rnl solve` gives a clock on `std::time::Instant`. A test can give a clock that advances by a step at each reading, so that the limit is reached at the same point on every run.
- The `Solution` places every object with a position of its own, the positions that the file fixes included, and orients every object that has an orientation; a text that its owner's symbol places has its position given when the scene gives it an axis of its own (13.3). But a text attached to a wire or a net, which has no coordinate point (13.3), or to anything else the solver has no point for, such as a terminal without a pin, and that no fact kept reaches, has none: the LP would leave it where the witness of the resolution happens to be, such as the origin, so the solution leaves it free, the viewer places it next to what it is attached to (13.3), above the middle of the wire, at the start of the net's first wire or on one of its pins, and a note of the report says how many. The record writes them all (the draft 0.3, 14.8), so that between two solves a statement of normal priority written after the record that conflicts with them is discarded; `write_record` leaves to the file only the axes on which a text follows what it is attached to, by an offset or by its symbol, where the solution has it there, and does not list as unsolved a position or an orientation of an object it cannot refer to that is what the facts of the file give it. It routes the ROUTEs (13.7): `wires`, each from a pin, a point of a wire written on the net or a branch point to another, `Bend`s between, with the ROUTE whose net and style it takes, the first ROUTE of the net; `branches`, the points where three wires or more meet, and the points on a pin where a wire routed meets another, each with a junction (13.2). `wire_routes` is empty: the `"auto"` wires written are not rewritten in this version.
- `SolverOptions`: the tolerance (11.7, `geom::TOL` by default), the grid free coordinates are rounded to (1), the layered objectives, the beam (4), the budget and the time limit (`time_limit`, seconds, `Some(5.0)` by default, `DEFAULT_TIME_LIMIT`; none for no limit). `SolverOptions::without_time_limit()` is the defaults without a limit: the resolution of 8.5 exactly, the same on every run, which every test of the toolkit uses. `hints_for_low` (false by default) asks for the hints of the facts of priority low: discarding such a fact is expected, and its hints may be left out (the draft 0.3, 8.5; 7.6), so without it a low fact discarded has only its first minimal conflict group, the one the deletion filter makes of the explanation of its conflict, with `complete` false, is in no hint by statement, and a note says that the groups of such facts are listed only with `rnl solve --report`, which sets it. Their groups can cost far more than the rest of the resolution: the low ORDER of corpus 03 alone took about 9 million resolve nodes. It changes no fact kept and no placement, only the report, so it is not in the canonical text and not in the hash, and `--solver-options` does not read it.
- `SolverOptions::canonical()`: every setting that changes the result, in a fixed order, numbers by `geom::fmt_num`. It is what the options digest of `sig1` hashes (chapter 17), so the same options give the same hash on every platform. `SolverOptions::parse` reads it back, and `with_overrides` reads some of its settings over other options, as `rnl solve --solver-options` does; an unknown key, a key given twice or a value that does not read is an error. The defaults:

  ```text
  tolerance=0.000001;grid=1;objectives=current_direction,turns,ground_direction,component_alignment,distribution,symmetry,terminal_wire_alignment,area,crossings,wire_length,aspect;beam=4;budget=candidates:256,astar_expansions:200000;time_limit=5
  ```

  `time_limit` is a number of seconds or `none`; it is part of the text, so a record solved without a limit has another hash than one solved with the default.

### Objectives

The objectives are configured in layers. Within a layer the objectives add up with their weights; layers rank lexicographically, the first first. A tolerance applies where candidates are ranked: the best is chosen layer by layer, the candidates within the layer's tolerance of its least value kept and the next layer deciding among them, the canonical code breaking what is left tied; then the best of the rest, and so on. Each layer is held against its least value, never one candidate against another, as "within a tolerance" is not transitive, so the order is well defined whatever the tolerances. The coordinate LP has linear layers of its own and keeps their optima exactly. An objective left out is off: it is not computed, does not order candidates and breaks no ties. In the canonical text the layers are separated by `,`, the objectives of a layer by `+`, a weight other than 1 follows `*`, and a tolerance other than 0 ends the layer after `~`, as in `signal_flow,area+wire_length*2~0.5`; `none` is no objective at all.

The objectives are `current_direction`, `ground_direction`, `signal_flow`, `component_alignment`, `distribution`, `symmetry`, `turns`, `crossings`, `wire_length`, `area`, `aspect`, `terminal_wire_alignment`, `labels` and `bend_alignment`. The default is one layer each, weight 1, in the prototype's order: `current_direction`, `turns`, `ground_direction`, `component_alignment`, `distribution`, `symmetry`, `terminal_wire_alignment`, `area`, `crossings`, `wire_length`, `aspect`.

### Budget

The search and the router count their work in steps, not time, so their result does not depend on the machine: `candidates`, the candidates of the search over the level, P0 always among them; `astar_expansions`, the cells the router expands for one net (`work.astar_expansions` is their sum over every net of every candidate routed). The witness of the resolution taken as written (see [Resolution](#resolution)) and the solver's own uses of the resolution, for the relations of a candidate, the analysis of the rows and the coordinate LP, are bounded by caps that the implementation fixes rather than options: 10 000 nodes for the search of one relation and 1 000 000 pivots for each simplex, backstops far above what a level of a few hundred objects needs (`work.pivots` is the sum over every simplex of the level, which no cap bounds). When a count runs out the solver keeps the best it has, at worst P0, and says so in the report: the solver's own relations left out without a conflict, symbols that overlap because a cap ran out, a net left in parts. The resolution of 8.5 has no budget of work: the time limit bounds it.

### Time limit

The draft 0.3 lets an implementation limit the time it spends on checking for a solution (8.5): deciding the facts is NP-hard in general, as some facts hold in one of several ways that must be tried in turn. `time_limit` is that limit for the resolution of one level, 5 seconds by default, `none` for no limit; the standard fixes no value.

- **What it covers**: the checks of the facts, each on its own first, then in their order, then a first minimal conflict group of each fact discarded, then the other groups of each, in that order, and nothing else. The witness taken as written after them is not timed but bounded by the caps of work, so it is the same whenever every fact is decided; nor are the structure, the search, the coordinates and the routing.
- **Where the time is read**: at the start of the check of each fact that the witness does not satisfy, at each node of a search, every 16 pivots of a simplex, at each seed of the enumeration of the groups and every 4096 nodes of the search of a seed. Once out, it stays out and the clock is no longer read.
- **A fact whose check does not finish** is discarded `undecided`, with no group and a warning (7.6). So is every later fact that needs a check; a later fact that the witness already satisfies is kept, as it needs no check, so the witness, and so the solution, still satisfies every fact kept. A fact whose check on its own does not finish is `undecided` too, not failed: nothing shows that it has no solution.
- **When the time runs out after every fact is decided**, the facts kept and the witness are those of 8.5. Each fact discarded has the groups found by then, each minimal, and `complete` false when they are not all: a group that its deletion filter had not made minimal is left out, as the hint names only minimal ones; `rnl solve` says on the line of the fact that the list is not complete. When a fact was left undecided, the witness is taken as written all the same, which is not timed, as the facts kept hold together: a `PLACE(5.5, 0)` decided before the time ran out puts its object at 5.5, not a tolerance off, so that a viewer reading the record back does not drop the author's own PLACE. Only when that cannot be done within the caps of work is the witness the one of the resolution, which satisfies the facts kept within the tolerance, and a note says so.
- **The result** may then differ from that of 8.5, and from one run to the next. Without a limit, and whenever no check reaches it, it is that of 8.5: with `time_limit=none` nothing changes against a solve without the option.

## Exact arithmetic

Every decision is taken with exact rationals (`num-rational` over `num-bigint`), never by comparing floating point, so native and WebAssembly builds decide alike.

- A number of the problem is read as its shortest decimal representation, the digits `fmt_num` writes, so `0.1` is 1/10. A result goes back as the nearest `f64`, ties to even, so a number that came in goes back unchanged.
- The tolerance (11.7) widens only the constant bounds of the facts: `x = v` becomes `v − tol ≤ x ≤ v + tol`, the `min` of DISTANCE is lowered and its `max` raised by it, and so is the one unit of ORDER; equalities between variables, such as ALIGN and DISTRIBUTE, stay exact.
- The simplex is incremental and bounded, after Dutertre and de Moura: each linear form is one row, made once; a fact is bounds on rows, each with the tag of the fact. A check moves the variable whose pivot costs least, one with no bound in its way first, then the one in the fewest rows, so a row added late, such as `t ≥ |xᵢ − xⱼ|` for the wire length, is fixed by one pivot on its own `t`; once variables have left the basis again 1000 times within one check it takes Bland's rule, which terminates. The same input gives the same pivots. When the bounds have no solution, the tags of the bounds that hold the failing row are the explanation, a set of facts that conflict. Bounds are undone back to a checkpoint, and the same tableau minimises linear forms for the layers of the coordinate LP, with Bland's rule, each objective a row whose reduced costs the pivots keep up to date.

The tests compare the simplex with complete deciders on systems made by a fixed pseudo-random sequence: Bellman–Ford on difference constraints, Gaussian elimination on equalities of three variables, Fourier–Motzkin elimination on small systems of any linear constraints and on the optimum of linear objectives. Each explanation is checked to have no solution by itself, whether a check pivots by cost or by Bland's rule, and a tableau whose bounds were asserted, undone and asserted again answers as one built afresh. One axis of a level of 150 objects, its facts taken in one by one and its wire-length rows made after them, checks in about one pivot per wire, as it does with those rows made first.

## Resolution

8.5 takes the facts in their order, `LevelProblem::facts`, and keeps each that fits with those kept before it. `resolve` does exactly that, with exact rationals:

- **The model** (`model`): on each axis, a variable for the origin of each object with a position of its own, for each pin and text placed by its symbol that a fact, a wire or a ROUTE reaches, for each centre of a set (6.5, 14.1), and for the separators and extremes of sets. A text whose symbol's position a PLACE or an offset in the scene replaces on one axis (13.3) is a point of its owner only on the other: on that axis its variable is its own coordinate, which its owner's orientation does not turn, and the facts on it do not move the owner. Each fact is one unit: bounds on linear forms, and choices where the fact is a disjunction. PLACE, a coordinate of a point and the `dx` or `dy` of a text give `x = v`; ALIGN equal members; DISTRIBUTE equal steps; ORDER neighbours at least one unit apart (14.3); DISTANCE bounds the gap between the extents of its two sides (14.5), a lower bound for every pair through a separator, an upper bound for one pair. The constant bounds are widened by the tolerance (11.7), never the equalities between variables.
- **Orientations** (`orient`): ORIENT and ORIENT-RELATION make classes in a union-find whose edges carry an orientation, `ori_to = ori_from.then(relation)` (14.9), each edge one fact, so a conflict is explained by the path that closes the cycle.
- **Literals**, the choices of the search: the direction of an ORDER given only an axis, the side of a DISTANCE given only an axis, the pair that keeps a gap of a DISTANCE between sets within its upper bound, the lowest and highest point of a set of three or more whose centre a fact constrains, and the orientation of a class once facts reach two points of one of its objects on one axis. Only then do the object's pins follow its orientation in the simplex; with one point reached, the origin takes up any orientation.
- **Facts that contradict themselves** (the draft 0.3, 8.5): each fact is first checked on its own, on a resolution with nothing taken in, taken in as below with every value of the literals it opens tried, the orientations of the classes it touches among them. A fact with no solution, such as `{#M1:d #M1:g} @ALIGN("y")` on an NMOS whose drain at (0, 2) and gate at (−3, 0) are at different heights whatever its orientation, an ORDER of an NMOS and the centre of its drain and its source, which is its origin whatever its orientation, or an ORDER of one set written twice, fails with an error (7.6) and takes no part in the resolution. The others are resolved on a model that leaves it out, its points and centres too, so that their resolution is that of the problem without it. Disabled facts never reach the problem (14.8). The check is of every fact the problem holds: those that a runtime can see contradict themselves from what is written alone, which `rnl-runtime` fails itself and leaves out of the problem (8.5, 9.5), and those that do only through the pins of a symbol and the orientations.
- **Taking a fact in** (`resolve`): the simplex holds the bounds of the facts kept and of every literal at the value the witness gives it. When the witness satisfies the new fact already, it is kept without a check. Otherwise one check with the literals as they are keeps it, or discards it when the conflict names no literal. When it names one, or the fact changes the orientation classes, a depth-first search over the literals, the witness's values first, checks the simplex at each node; a conflict whose explanation names no open literal fails the node, otherwise the search decides one of the literals it names (conflict-directed backjumping), and the explanations with literals become nogoods that prune later searches. Orientations that give the links the same constants are tried once. The search is complete, so a fact is discarded only when no values of the literals fit.
- **Conflict groups**: the first is the facts of the explanation, made minimal by a deletion filter, the facts of the latest rank left out first; each test is a small resolution of its own, and a test that still conflicts shrinks the set to its own explanation. A conflict that needs a long chain of facts costs a test per fact, each taking the chain in again. The others are enumerated by MARCO (Liffiton et al.) over the same filter, once every fact discarded has its first: a map of the subsets of the facts kept before the fact not yet explored gives a seed, a largest subset that holds no group found whole and is no subset of a set found to fit, found by a search with backtracking that takes the facts that rank first in first. A seed that fits with the fact is a largest set that does, and every later seed must hold a fact outside it; a seed that does not holds a new group, which the filter makes minimal, and every later seed must leave out a fact of it. When no seed is left the groups are all; each costs about one resolution per group and per largest set that fits, and their filters. A fact of priority low keeps its first group only unless `hints_for_low` asks for its hints (8.5, 7.6), as its groups can be many. The seeds come from one search over the facts, each in or out, the facts that rank first taken in first, with unit propagation over the two kinds of clauses of the map (a group of which every fact but one is in leaves that one out; a set found to fit outside which every fact but one is out takes that one in), which goes on from each seed to the next instead of starting again: the seed is the same, and `k` independent conflicts, with their 2^k largest sets that fit, take a few thousand nodes for `k` = 8 rather than a million.
- **Time limit**: the resolution stops deciding when the time limit is out (see [Time limit](#time-limit)): a fact whose check did not finish is discarded `undecided`, with a warning. Deciding the facts is NP-hard in general (the side of `"x"` DISTANCEs alone can encode graph colouring), so a level can need it.
- **The witness** satisfies every fact kept. It is taken from the facts kept resolved once more without the tolerance when they hold so, so that a PLACE at 4 puts its object at 4; otherwise with as many of their constants as written as hold together, in the order of 8.5. These resolutions are counted by the caps of work, not timed, and are made when a fact was left undecided too; when they cannot be made within the caps, the witness is the resolution's, within the tolerance, and a note says so.

## Objectives

Each objective has a row in a table (`objectives`): its default scope, within groups (11.5) or over the level, the inputs it needs, whether it has a structural side, and how it is measured. Every value is an exact rational.

| Objective | Measures (smaller is better) | Structural side | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `current_direction` | MOS and BJT whose source (emitter) is not on the side of the ground | rows by current depth; sources towards the ground; a two-terminal element with an end on a supply or the ground hung towards it, one between two signals laid across | a supply and a ground symbol |
| `ground_direction` | ground symbols whose pin is not above their body | grounds hang below their pins; a two-terminal element with an end on the ground hung with that end down | a ground symbol |
| `signal_flow` | pairs of an input port right of an output port | inputs left, outputs right | a port whose kind can be told |
| `component_alignment` | distinct x plus distinct y of the components, per group | connected elements in one column; barycentre order | |
| `distribution` | the prototype's gap kinds of the components' coordinates, per group | rows evenly spaced | |
| `symmetry` | mirror pairs not symmetric | mirrored orientations, the first of a pair on the left | |
| `turns` | bends; without wires, each connection of a net routed as the router draws it, a pin leaving by a lead of a unit its own way: none for a straight line, one for an L, three for a jog (an end leaving across the connection with the other not beyond its lead), two for a U (both ends leaving the same way, across the connection or off an axis), four for an S, two more for each end leaving away from the other; for a wire written, an L for each connection not along an axis and a turn at each pin a connection does not leave along | | something drawn as wires |
| `crossings` | crossings of wires of different nets | barycentre order | something drawn as wires |
| `wire_length` | the length of the wires; without wires, of a minimum spanning tree of each net | barycentre order | something drawn as wires |
| `area`, `aspect` | the envelope's area; the distance of its width over height from 16/9 | | |

`terminal_wire_alignment`, `labels` and `bend_alignment` are not computed by this version. An objective configured but inert in a level, one this version does not compute or one whose inputs the level lacks, is treated as off, and `inert_objectives` says why in one sentence. An objective that is off makes no candidate, ranks none and breaks no tie; ties go to the canonical code of the candidates.

## Structure, search and coordinates

- **Structure** (`structure`): what each object is (an element of the rows, a supply, ground or port symbol, a point, a text; the device, from the symbol: NMOS, PMOS, NPN, PNP with the pins in SPICE's order); the nets as far as the problem shows them (ROUTEs, every ROUTE of a net merged, wires, usages attached to nets) and what is drawn as wires; supply and ground from the usages of `$SUPPLY_STUB`, `$GND_STUB`, `$SUPPLY` and `$GND`, the nets they attach to, the terminals they hang from or are drawn on (12.1) and the nets of those terminals; the current depth by breadth-first search from a supply terminal through the pins the current runs through; the tree of groups, nested by containment, two overlapping groups split in three with the shared part in the middle; the orientation classes no ORIENT fixes; the rows the elements start in (by current depth, or by the heights the facts give), an ALIGN on y joining rows and, without the current depth, an ORDER or a DISTRIBUTE on x too where the facts let its elements share a height, an ALIGN on x making a column, its elements in rows of their own; the left-to-right precedences of the facts on x that fix a side (an ORDER with a direction, a DISTANCE with a direction and a gap not below 0), the members of a set at its centre, and those of an ORDER or a DISTANCE whose side is left open, which the search may reverse; the sources of candidates the configured objectives call for. The problem carries no netlist of its own, so a net neither routed nor wired is known only through the usages on it.
- **Candidates** (`search`): the orientation of every free class, the rows and the order in them, the groups kept apart as blocks, and the relations that follow: neighbours in a row held on their heights by the pins that connect them, as the wire between them is drawn: pins that leave towards each other on one line; a pin that leaves sideways towards one with no direction on its line; a pin that leaves sideways towards one that leaves up or down at least a unit beyond that one's lead, an inequality, so that the router's wire is an L rather than a jog of three bends; two pins that both leave up, or both down, joined by the router, on one line, a rail; a wire written that its mode draws straight keeps its pins on one line, one that its mode draws as an L from the pin that leaves up or down takes the inequality; otherwise by their origins; and apart by their boxes and the texts their symbols place, a pin that leaves right and one that leaves left on two nets routed three apart, so that their leads do not end on one node, neighbouring rows apart, blocks apart, ports beside the pins they connect to (a port that the router joins to a pin that leaves up or down two beyond the pin that way, in the gap between two rows when the net has a pin facing that one from the next row, so that its wire is an L or a T in the middle of the wire between those pins; a port on a net written, or drawn by nothing, at the pin's height), and, from the objectives, columns and even rows. Each relation is a unit of the solver's own, taken in by the resolution after every fact kept and dropped when it does not fit; no fact is ever dropped for one.
- **P0** is the baseline: rows by depth, the order of the netlist, every free orientation R0, ports beside what they connect to, stubs where their facts put them.
- **The beam search** goes up the tree of groups: the children's variants combined, the order of the items by barycentres until it settles and then neighbours swapped and each fact whose side is open reversed, each class turned so that most of its pins leave towards what they connect to, among the orientations its sources allow; variants ranked by the layered estimates on a sketch of the level, the best of each order kept first, then checked by the resolution with the node's relations; `beam` variants kept. The root's variants, P0, and with even rows in force a twin of each, are placed, and of those with the fewest overlapping symbols the first by the layered score, as the objectives rank it, is taken.
- **Coordinates** (`coords`): the orientations taken in first and read back, then the relations made for them; on each axis every pin and text linked to its origin, then a lexicographic LP: the half perimeter of each net drawn as wires, a pin of a net routed counted by the end of its lead, a unit its own way, the envelope of the components, the distance to where the resolution put each origin, or, for a text attached to a point or to an object with a coordinate point, to where it goes when no fact places it: at the point, or beside the object, as a viewer that does not solve puts it (13.3); for a text attached to a wire, beside its first point, and for one attached to a net, beside the first point of its first wire, else of its first ROUTE, else its first pin, which counts only on the axis that no fact reaches of such a text that a fact reaches on the other. Each optimum is kept for the next layer by fixing the variables its row holds at a bound (their reduced costs are not 0), which adds no row. The constants of the relations are on the grid, those that put overlapping symbols apart included. Then the origins go on the grid, the symbols first in the order of the objects and then the points and texts: each to the nearer grid point beside it, or the other when the bounds do not allow the nearer, and held where it ends up on the grid so that a later one cannot move it off; a symbol that neither fits is tried once more first, the origins held before it held again on the grid after it, as a centre is tied to its set. What still does not fit stays where the facts force it, and the layers are solved again with the origins moved kept. Two symbols that still overlap get a relation with four sides to choose from, ranked after the facts and before the structure; a note on those that still overlap says why: the facts kept with the orientations taken leave no side, the symbols put apart before them leave none, the rounds ran out, or a cap of work did.

## Routing and scoring

- **The candidates routed** (`driver`): every candidate placed is ranked by the estimate of the objectives, the routing objectives measured on the straight connections of each net; the best `beam` are routed and scored on their wires, and the best of those is the solution.
- **Routing** (`route`), a deterministic rectilinear A* on a lattice of the level: the coordinate of every pin and of every point and bend of a wire written, so that every pin is a node, and the integers over the extent and a margin within 8 units of one of those or of an edge of a box, the tracks near the symbols; between coordinates farther apart the lattice goes on in one edge, so that a symbol placed far away does not make it larger. Lengths are exact, the rationals scaled to integers by their common denominator. The obstacles are the boxes of the placed symbols turned with them, widened by one unit; a point the net joins reaches the free lattice by a lead, a straight run out of the widened boxes through no symbol's box and past no other pin, one that is not the pin's own way (9.2.4) costing a violated exit; the lead a pin's own way is kept for its net. A lead along the edge of the pin's own symbol is a fallback for a pin whose own way is taken (by the pin of another net facing it across a gap of 2, or by the wire of a stub written on it), at a cost of 8 more, and a path runs through it only straight. A wire may cross another straight, at a cost, but never runs along one or touches its bends and ends (13.1, Principles 2.2); the wires written are drawn by their mode, `"auto"` straight, those on the net being routed part of its tree by their points. A net's points, pins sorted by x and then y, are in parts that its wires written join; the two nearest parts are joined first, then each part left, the nearest first, to the nearest point of the tree, any point of a wire made for the net a place to branch off; a part with no open lead is reported without a search, and a part that found no path is tried again once the tree has grown. The cost of a path is its length, 2 for each bend, 4 for each crossing, 2 for each end that violates its pin's exit and 8 for each lead along an edge. A wire that meets one made before in its middle splits it there: a branch point, where three wires meet, with a junction (13.2); where a wire routed ends at a pin at which another wire ends, routed or written, the wires routed end at a branch point with a junction on the pin, which a wire of length 0 joins to it. The nets are routed the smallest first, by the half perimeter of their points. A part with no path to the rest, or a net whose budget ran out, is left as it is and reported; so is a net whose ROUTEs give different styles, whose wires take the first's.
- **Scoring** (`score`): the objectives measured on the wires routed and on the wires written, drawn by their mode, a wire written on a net that a ROUTE asks for counting as of that net. Candidates rank by the pairs of symbols that overlap, then the places where the wires mislead, then the joins routing left undone, then the layers within their tolerances, the canonical code breaking ties: the solver's own rules rank after every fact and before every objective (8.5, 11.7), and a ROUTE is a request of the file where an objective is a preference of the solver. Lines that touch or overlap make no connection (13.2), so the wires mislead where two segments of different nets touch (an end or a bend of one on the other, or a stretch of one line shared), where a pin is on a wire of another net, and where a wire runs through the inside of a symbol's box (but to a pin of that symbol inside its box); a wire of a bus is on each of its nets. The wires routed never do; the count is for the wires written, which their mode draws between the points the placement moves, and before routing it ranks the candidates by those alone. It picks among the candidates and does not move them: where every candidate has such a wire, the best of them still does. A pin's net is known only from what the level draws, its ROUTE, a wire written on that one net that ends at it, or a usage that attaches its symbol to the net (12.1): the problem holds no connections of the netlist, so a wire written over a pin of a net that nothing draws is not counted.

In `simplex`, a check stopped by its caller goes on from where it stopped, and a caller that never stops it changes nothing.

The crate does no file I/O, reads no clock and takes no randomness, so it builds for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`; the clock of the time limit is the caller's. Without a time limit, and whenever no check reaches it, the same input and options give the same output on every platform. Its dependencies are pure Rust: the runtime and the `num` crates.

## Not yet done

Solver v1 leaves these out; the interfaces have room for them.

- **Templates, channels and the feedback of the routes** (5.5 and 5.6 of the synthesis of the brainstorm, `notes/solver-design/algorithm-synthesis.md` in rnl-dev): the ROUTEs are routed by A* alone, net by net, once the coordinates are fixed. No wire template is chosen while the candidates are searched, no channels or tracks are assigned between rows, the bends the router finds are not fed back to the coordinate LP for another round, and the nets of a symmetric group are not routed as mirror images. The `"auto"` wires written are drawn straight and not rewritten (`Solution::wire_routes` is empty, the draft 0.3, 13.1).
- **The geometry of the wires written in the placement**: the coordinate LP measures a wire written by the extent of its points, not by the path its mode draws, so it may put two wires of different nets on one line or a wire through a symbol. The order of candidates counts that ([Pipeline](#pipeline), scoring) and takes a candidate without it when there is one, but nothing moves a candidate to get one, and the candidates do not try the orientations or the places that would avoid it. In the conformance corpus, wires written still run through symbols in levels of 03, 04, 05, 07, 08, 09, 10, 11, 12 and 15, mostly `"straight"` wires between pins that the placement puts apart (in 10, the straight wires between the resistors of a row run diagonally from the bottom of one to the top of the next, where resistors turned on their side would join them straight), and touch a wire or a pin of another net in 03, 10 and 12; the port of `a` in `inverter-chain`'s `inv` is placed with its pin on the wire of `y`.
- **Warm start and drag projection** (5.9 of the synthesis): every solve starts from the problem alone. The previous record of the solver is not read (2.8), so a solve does not stay near the last one, and an editor's drag is not projected onto the facts; it takes effect as the editor's record is read, as for T2b.
- **Polish** (5.8 of the synthesis): no one-step neighbourhood search improves the candidate taken.
- **What misleads, ranked and configured**: the count of the places where the wires mislead ([Pipeline](#pipeline), scoring) is a rule of the solver's own, not one of the objectives: it ranks after the overlaps and before the joins left undone and every objective, and cannot be moved among the layers or turned off. So a candidate that leaves a ROUTE in parts ranks before one routed whole whose wire written touches another net. Whether it should rank after the joins left undone, or be a layer of the objectives that is on by default, is not decided. It knows the net of a pin only from what the level draws (see scoring).
- **Labels**: no placement of labels or texts beyond what their facts and symbols say; `labels`, `terminal_wire_alignment` and `bend_alignment` are inert (see [Objectives](#objectives)).
- **WebAssembly**: the crate builds for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`, but there is no binding (`bindings/solver-wasm`), no check that native and WebAssembly builds give the same solutions, and the viewer does not solve on demand.
- **What a file of 0.2 cannot hold**: the record of a file of 0.2 cannot refer to an object without a name, such as the middle point of a `$TAIL_CENTER` written without a name. The solver decides it all the same; `Written::unsolved` lists it, it stays without a position, and the constraints that reach it cannot be checked. The draft 0.3 writes it by number (5.9). T3 and T4 name their `$TAIL_CENTER` (`%tc`), so that their records of 0.2 place its middle point as `%tc::mid`.
- **The place of a usage**: the problem of a level has no place for a usage, so a statement in the body of a definition is named by where it is written there and by its usage, with the roles as the level writes them, not by the line of its AS or ATTACH statement.

See the [roadmap](../../docs/roadmap.md) for planned work.
