# rnl-solver

The solver of RNL descriptions. It takes the problem of one drawing level, the `LevelProblem` that the runtime builds when it reads a document with `Options::solver()` (see [For a solver](../rnl-runtime/README.md#for-a-solver)), resolves its geometric facts as 8.5 says, places and orients its objects and routes its ROUTEs (13.7), and gives a `Solution`, which the runtime writes back as the level's record of the solver:

```rust
let solved = rnl_solver::solve(problem, &options);
let hash = rnl_runtime::problem::sig1(problem, &options.canonical());
let written = rnl_runtime::solution::write_record(problem, &solved.solution, rnl_solver::AUTHOR, &hash);
```

**Status: stage S4 of solver v1.** The crate, its options, its exact arithmetic, the resolution of 8.5, the structure of a level, the search, the coordinates, the routing and the layered score are in place: `resolve` keeps or discards each fact, exactly, with minimal conflict sets; `solve` then searches candidates structure first, places each by a lexicographic LP rounded to the grid, every fact kept holding exactly, routes the ROUTEs of the best of them around the symbols, with branch points and junctions, and takes the best by the layered objectives in force on their wires. The stages that follow are the `rnl solve` command (S5) and the end-to-end demonstration (S6).

## Interface

- `resolve(&LevelProblem, &SolverOptions) -> Resolved`: the resolution of 8.5 alone (see [Resolution](#resolution)): `kept`, `discarded` as in the report below, the `work` done, `notes`, and the witness, read with `position(object)`, `orientation(object)` and `point(problem, &PointRef)`.
- `solve(&LevelProblem, &SolverOptions) -> Solved`: the `Solution`, and a `SolveReport` of what became of each fact. 8.5 takes the facts in the order of `LevelProblem::facts`, which is their rank, and keeps each that fits with those kept before it. `kept` lists the facts kept; `discarded` each fact discarded, with `conflicts_with`, a minimal set of facts kept before it that it conflicts with, the facts that rank first preferred, and `undecided` when the budget for deciding it ran out. `inert_objectives` lists the objectives that are on but have nothing to work with; `unrouted` the ROUTEs whose nets the router left in parts (`Unrouted`: the ROUTE, the net, the parts, whether its budget ran out), which is not an error, the viewer drawing flight lines for what is left; `work` counts what was done, as the budget counts it.
- The `Solution` places every object with a position of its own and orients every object that has an orientation; it routes the ROUTEs (13.7): `wires`, each from a pin, a point of a wire written on the net or a branch point to another, `Bend`s between, with the ROUTE whose net and style it takes, the first ROUTE of the net; `branches`, the points where three wires or more meet, each with a junction (13.2). `wire_routes` is empty: the `"auto"` wires written are not rewritten in this version.
- `SolverOptions`: the tolerance (11.7, `geom::TOL` by default), the grid free coordinates are rounded to (1), the layered objectives, the beam (4) and the budget.
- `SolverOptions::canonical()`: every setting that changes the result, in a fixed order, numbers by `geom::fmt_num`. It is what the options digest of `sig1` hashes (chapter 17), so the same options give the same hash on every platform. `SolverOptions::parse` reads it back, and `with_overrides` reads some of its settings over other options:

  ```text
  tolerance=0.000001;grid=1;objectives=current_direction,turns,ground_direction,component_alignment,distribution,symmetry,terminal_wire_alignment,area,crossings,wire_length,aspect;beam=4;budget=resolve_nodes:10000,pivots:1000000,candidates:256,astar_expansions:200000
  ```

### Objectives

The objectives are configured in layers. Within a layer the objectives add up with their weights; layers rank lexicographically, the first first. A tolerance applies where candidates are ranked: the best is chosen layer by layer, the candidates within the layer's tolerance of its least value kept and the next layer deciding among them, the canonical code breaking what is left tied; then the best of the rest, and so on. Each layer is held against its least value, never one candidate against another, as "within a tolerance" is not transitive, so the order is well defined whatever the tolerances. The coordinate LP has linear layers of its own and keeps their optima exactly. An objective left out is off: it is not computed, does not order candidates and breaks no ties. In the canonical text the layers are separated by `,`, the objectives of a layer by `+`, a weight other than 1 follows `*`, and a tolerance other than 0 ends the layer after `~`, as in `signal_flow,area+wire_length*2~0.5`; `none` is no objective at all.

The objectives are `current_direction`, `ground_direction`, `signal_flow`, `component_alignment`, `distribution`, `symmetry`, `turns`, `crossings`, `wire_length`, `area`, `aspect`, `terminal_wire_alignment`, `labels` and `bend_alignment`. The default is one layer each, weight 1, in the prototype's order: `current_direction`, `turns`, `ground_direction`, `component_alignment`, `distribution`, `symmetry`, `terminal_wire_alignment`, `area`, `crossings`, `wire_length`, `aspect`.

### Budget

Work is counted in steps, not time, so the result does not depend on the machine: `resolve_nodes`, the nodes of the search that decides one fact; `pivots`, the pivots of each simplex the solver builds (one per axis for the resolution, for each test of a conflict set, for the rows, and for each candidate taken in and placed; `work.pivots` is their sum over the level, which the budget does not bound); `candidates`, the candidates of the search over the level, P0 always among them; `astar_expansions`, the cells the router expands for one net (`work.astar_expansions` is their sum over every net of every candidate routed). When a count runs out the solver keeps the best it has, at worst P0, and says so in the report: a fact left undecided, the solver's own relations left out without a conflict, symbols that overlap because the budget ran out, a net left in parts.

## Exact arithmetic

Every decision is taken with exact rationals (`num-rational` over `num-bigint`), never by comparing floating point, so native and WebAssembly builds decide alike.

- A number of the problem is read as its shortest decimal representation, the digits `fmt_num` writes, so `0.1` is 1/10. A result goes back as the nearest `f64`, ties to even, so a number that came in goes back unchanged.
- The tolerance (11.7) widens only the constant bounds of the facts: `x = v` becomes `v − tol ≤ x ≤ v + tol`, the `min` of DISTANCE is lowered and its `max` raised by it, and so is the one unit of ORDER; equalities between variables, such as ALIGN and DISTRIBUTE, stay exact.
- The simplex is incremental and bounded, after Dutertre and de Moura: each linear form is one row, made once; a fact is bounds on rows, each with the tag of the fact. A check moves the variable whose pivot costs least, one with no bound in its way first, then the one in the fewest rows, so a row added late, such as `t ≥ |xᵢ − xⱼ|` for the wire length, is fixed by one pivot on its own `t`; once variables have left the basis again 1000 times within one check it takes Bland's rule, which terminates. The same input gives the same pivots. When the bounds have no solution, the tags of the bounds that hold the failing row are the explanation, a set of facts that conflict. Bounds are undone back to a checkpoint, and the same tableau minimises linear forms for the layers of the coordinate LP, with Bland's rule, each objective a row whose reduced costs the pivots keep up to date.

The tests compare the simplex with complete deciders on systems made by a fixed pseudo-random sequence: Bellman–Ford on difference constraints, Gaussian elimination on equalities of three variables, Fourier–Motzkin elimination on small systems of any linear constraints and on the optimum of linear objectives. Each explanation is checked to have no solution by itself, whether a check pivots by cost or by Bland's rule, and a tableau whose bounds were asserted, undone and asserted again answers as one built afresh. One axis of a level of 150 objects, its facts taken in one by one and its wire-length rows made after them, checks in about one pivot per wire, as it does with those rows made first.

## Resolution

8.5 takes the facts in their order, `LevelProblem::facts`, and keeps each that fits with those kept before it. `resolve` does exactly that, with exact rationals:

- **The model** (`model`): on each axis, a variable for the origin of each object with a position of its own, for each pin and text placed by its symbol that a fact, a wire or a ROUTE reaches, for each centre of a set (6.5, 14.1), and for the separators and extremes of sets. Each fact is one unit: bounds on linear forms, and choices where the fact is a disjunction. PLACE, a coordinate of a point and the `dx` or `dy` of a text give `x = v`; ALIGN equal members; DISTRIBUTE equal steps; ORDER neighbours at least one unit apart (14.3); DISTANCE bounds the gap between the extents of its two sides (14.5), a lower bound for every pair through a separator, an upper bound for one pair. The constant bounds are widened by the tolerance (11.7), never the equalities between variables.
- **Orientations** (`orient`): ORIENT and ORIENT-RELATION make classes in a union-find whose edges carry an orientation, `ori_to = ori_from.then(relation)` (14.9), each edge one fact, so a conflict is explained by the path that closes the cycle.
- **Literals**, the choices of the search: the direction of an ORDER given only an axis, the side of a DISTANCE given only an axis, the pair that keeps a gap of a DISTANCE between sets within its upper bound, the lowest and highest point of a set of three or more whose centre a fact constrains, and the orientation of a class once facts reach two points of one of its objects on one axis. Only then do the object's pins follow its orientation in the simplex; with one point reached, the origin takes up any orientation.
- **Taking a fact in** (`resolve`): the simplex holds the bounds of the facts kept and of every literal at the value the witness gives it. When the witness satisfies the new fact already, it is kept without a check. Otherwise one check with the literals as they are keeps it, or discards it when the conflict names no literal. When it names one, or the fact changes the orientation classes, a depth-first search over the literals, the witness's values first, checks the simplex at each node; a conflict whose explanation names no open literal fails the node, otherwise the search decides one of the literals it names (conflict-directed backjumping), and the explanations with literals become nogoods that prune later searches. Orientations that give the links the same constants are tried once. The search is complete, so a fact is discarded only when no values of the literals fit.
- **Conflict sets**: the facts of the explanation, made minimal by a deletion filter, the facts of the latest rank left out first; each test is a small resolution of its own, and a test that still conflicts shrinks the set to its own explanation. A conflict that needs a long chain of facts costs a test per fact, each taking the chain in again.
- **Budget**: a search stops after `resolve_nodes` nodes; its fact is then discarded and reported `undecided`, with a note. Deciding the facts is NP-hard in general (the side of `"x"` DISTANCEs alone can encode graph colouring), so a level can need it; the standard does not say yet what a solver does then.
- **The witness** satisfies every fact kept. It is taken from the facts kept resolved once more without the tolerance when they hold so, so that a PLACE at 4 puts its object at 4.

## Objectives

Each objective has a row in a table (`objectives`): its default scope, within groups (11.5) or over the level, the inputs it needs, whether it has a structural side, and how it is measured. Every value is an exact rational.

| Objective | Measures (smaller is better) | Structural side | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `current_direction` | MOS and BJT whose source (emitter) is not on the side of the ground | rows by current depth; sources towards the ground; a two-terminal element with an end on a supply or the ground hung towards it, one between two signals laid across | a supply and a ground symbol |
| `ground_direction` | ground symbols whose pin is not above their body | grounds hang below their pins | a ground symbol |
| `signal_flow` | pairs of an input port right of an output port | inputs left, outputs right | a port whose kind can be told |
| `component_alignment` | distinct x plus distinct y of the components, per group | connected elements in one column; barycentre order | |
| `distribution` | the prototype's gap kinds of the components' coordinates, per group | rows evenly spaced | |
| `symmetry` | mirror pairs not symmetric | mirrored orientations, the first of a pair on the left | |
| `turns` | bends; without wires, an L for each connection not along an axis and a turn at each pin a connection does not leave along | | something drawn as wires |
| `crossings` | crossings of wires of different nets | barycentre order | something drawn as wires |
| `wire_length` | the length of the wires; without wires, of a minimum spanning tree of each net | barycentre order | something drawn as wires |
| `area`, `aspect` | the envelope's area; the distance of its width over height from 16/9 | | |

`terminal_wire_alignment`, `labels` and `bend_alignment` are not computed by this version. An objective configured but inert in a level, one this version does not compute or one whose inputs the level lacks, is treated as off, and `inert_objectives` says why in one sentence. An objective that is off makes no candidate, ranks none and breaks no tie; ties go to the canonical code of the candidates.

## Structure, search and coordinates

- **Structure** (`structure`): what each object is (an element of the rows, a supply, ground or port symbol, a point, a text; the device, from the symbol: NMOS, PMOS, NPN, PNP with the pins in SPICE's order); the nets as far as the problem shows them (ROUTEs, every ROUTE of a net merged, wires, usages attached to nets) and what is drawn as wires; supply and ground from the usages of `$SUPPLY_STUB`, `$GND_STUB`, `$SUPPLY` and `$GND`, the nets they attach to, the terminals they hang from or are drawn on (12.1) and the nets of those terminals; the current depth by breadth-first search from a supply terminal through the pins the current runs through; the tree of groups, nested by containment, two overlapping groups split in three with the shared part in the middle; the orientation classes no ORIENT fixes; the rows the elements start in (by current depth, or by the heights the facts give), an ALIGN on y joining rows and, without the current depth, an ORDER or a DISTRIBUTE on x too where the facts let its elements share a height, an ALIGN on x making a column, its elements in rows of their own; the left-to-right precedences of the facts on x that fix a side (an ORDER with a direction, a DISTANCE with a direction and a gap not below 0), the members of a set at its centre, and those of an ORDER or a DISTANCE whose side is left open, which the search may reverse; the sources of candidates the configured objectives call for. The problem carries no netlist of its own, so a net neither routed nor wired is known only through the usages on it.
- **Candidates** (`search`): the orientation of every free class, the rows and the order in them, the groups kept apart as blocks, and the relations that follow: neighbours in a row on one line (by the pins that connect them when one leaves sideways, otherwise by their origins) and apart by their boxes and the texts their symbols place, neighbouring rows apart, blocks apart, ports beside the pins they connect to, and, from the objectives, columns and even rows. Each relation is a unit of the solver's own, taken in by the resolution after every fact kept and dropped when it does not fit; no fact is ever dropped for one.
- **P0** is the baseline: rows by depth, the order of the netlist, every free orientation R0, ports beside what they connect to, stubs where their facts put them.
- **The beam search** goes up the tree of groups: the children's variants combined, the order of the items by barycentres until it settles and then neighbours swapped and each fact whose side is open reversed, each class turned so that most of its pins leave towards what they connect to, among the orientations its sources allow; variants ranked by the layered estimates on a sketch of the level, the best of each order kept first, then checked by the resolution with the node's relations; `beam` variants kept. The root's variants, P0, and with even rows in force a twin of each, are placed, and of those with the fewest overlapping symbols the first by the layered score, as the objectives rank it, is taken.
- **Coordinates** (`coords`): the orientations taken in first and read back, then the relations made for them; on each axis every pin and text linked to its origin, then a lexicographic LP: the half perimeter of each net drawn as wires, the envelope of the components, the distance to where the resolution put each origin. Each optimum is kept for the next layer by fixing the variables its row holds at a bound (their reduced costs are not 0), which adds no row. The constants of the relations are on the grid, those that put overlapping symbols apart included. Then the origins go on the grid, the symbols first in the order of the objects and then the points and texts: each to the nearer grid point beside it, or the other when the bounds do not allow the nearer, and held where it ends up on the grid so that a later one cannot move it off; a symbol that neither fits is tried once more first, the origins held before it held again on the grid after it, as a centre is tied to its set. What still does not fit stays where the facts force it, and the layers are solved again with the origins moved kept. Two symbols that still overlap get a relation with four sides to choose from, ranked after the facts and before the structure; a note on those that still overlap says why: the facts kept with the orientations taken leave no side, the symbols put apart before them leave none, the rounds ran out, or the budget did.

## Routing and scoring

- **The candidates routed** (`driver`): every candidate placed is ranked by the estimate of the objectives, the routing objectives measured on the straight connections of each net; the best `beam` are routed and scored on their wires, and the best of those is the solution.
- **Routing** (`route`), a deterministic rectilinear A* on a lattice of the level: every integer coordinate over its extent and a margin, and the coordinate of every pin and of every point and bend of a wire written, so that every pin is a node; lengths exact, the rationals scaled to integers by their common denominator. The obstacles are the boxes of the placed symbols turned with them, widened by one unit; a point the net joins reaches the free lattice by a lead, a straight run out of the widened boxes through no symbol's box and past no other pin, one that is not the pin's own way (9.2.4) costing a violated exit and leaving the box of its symbol at once; the lead a pin's own way is kept for its net. A wire may cross another straight, at a cost, but never runs along one or touches its bends and ends (13.1, Principles 2.2); the wires written are drawn by their mode, `"auto"` straight. A net's points, pins sorted by x and then y, are in parts that its wires written join; the two nearest parts are joined first, then each part left, the nearest first, to the nearest point of the tree, any point of a wire made for the net a place to branch off. The cost of a path is its length, 2 for each bend, 4 for each crossing and 2 for each end that violates its pin's exit. A wire that meets one made before in its middle splits it there: a branch point, where three wires meet, with a junction (13.2). The nets are routed the smallest first, by the half perimeter of their points. A part with no path to the rest, or a net whose budget ran out, is left as it is and reported.
- **Scoring** (`score`): the objectives measured on the wires routed and on the wires written, drawn by their mode, a wire written on a net that a ROUTE asks for counting as of that net. Candidates rank by the pairs of symbols that overlap, then the joins routing left undone, then the layers within their tolerances, the canonical code breaking ties: the solver's own rules rank after every fact and before every objective (8.5, 11.7), and a ROUTE is a request of the file where an objective is a preference of the solver.

The public interface is `solve` and `resolve` and what they take and give: the options (`SolverOptions`, `Objectives`, `Layer`, `Budget`), the names of the objectives and the default order of the layers (`OBJECTIVES`, `DEFAULT_LAYERS`), the report types (`Solved`, `SolveReport`, `Discarded`, `Unrouted`, `Work`), the resolution (`Resolved`), the `AUTHOR` the solver writes in its record, and, re-exported from the runtime, `LevelProblem` and `Solution`. The modules are private.

The tests of the search and the coordinates (`driver`) read every level of T1–T6 and of the conformance corpus from the files and solve it: every fact kept holds where the objects are, checked exactly on the rationals before they become `f64`; two instances overlap only when no side can put them apart with the facts kept; a solve is the same twice; every object with a position of its own is placed and every orientation given. T3 is drawn as the development plan pictures it: the mirror on top with the gates facing each other, the pair in the middle, M5 under the midpoint, the supplies above, the ground below, the ports beside the gates and the drain they connect to. T6, without a supply, reports the current direction inert. With other options (no objective, no grid, a beam of one, no budget for candidates, tolerances on the layers, a budget of 40 pivots) the facts still hold. Every ROUTE of T1–T6 is routed whole, and on every level of T1–T6 and of the corpus each wire runs from a point of its net or a branch point to another, along the axes and through no symbol's box, each branch point is shared by three wires or more with a junction, and each net is one tree unless the report says in how many parts it is left; a budget too small for anything (two nodes, 30 pivots, no candidates, 12 expansions) still gives such a solution. Small netlists given as text check the rest: a bound off the grid puts what it moves on the next grid point, a text half a unit off leaves its symbol on the grid, a supply and a ground drawn on terminals give the current depth, an ORDER on x is a row and an ALIGN on x a column, an ORDER given only an axis fixes no precedence, and an overlap is noted with its cause.

The tests of `route` route layouts given as plain geometry: two pins that face each other, a symbol in the way, a third pin that branches off with a junction and a fourth at the same point, pins that touch (a wire of length 0, 13.1), a crossing taken straight rather than a long way round, no running along another wire, the exit of a pin, a wire written that is part of the tree, a pin walled in, a budget that runs out, coordinates off the integers, the lead of a pin on its symbol. `tests/solve.rs` solves every level of T1–T6 through the public interface, writes its record into the file with `write_record`, `finish_record` and `replace_generated`, and reads the file again as a viewer does: no error, nothing unplaced, no flight line left, a straight wire for each wire routed, a junction for each branch point, and the hash still the same.

The tests of `resolve` include the examples of the development plan, an orientation that matters only once two points of an object are reached, a randomized check that the explanations of the orientation classes are minimal, and, in `tests/resolve.rs`, every level of the cases written for the solver (`tests/integration/solver`) and of the conformance corpus, read from the files: every fact kept holds at the witness as a viewer checks it, every conflict set conflicts and is minimal, and T4 resolves as its reference says.

The crate does no file I/O and takes no time or randomness, so it builds for `wasm32-unknown-unknown` and the same input and options give the same output on every platform. Its dependencies are pure Rust: the runtime and the `num` crates.
