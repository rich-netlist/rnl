# 0007. The core ships Noto Sans SC and lays out text with its metrics

- Date: 2026-10-14
- Status: accepted

## Context

[Decision 0006](0006-shared-core-per-platform-frontends.md) makes fonts part of the core's drawing contract: the core lays out text with the metrics of fonts shipped with it, and a frontend only rasterizes the primitives with those fonts. It left the font set and its licence to be chosen. Until now the renderer estimated widths per class of character, and the SVG placed texts with `text-anchor`, baselines estimated from the size and the browser's sub- and superscripts, in the font the style names or the platform's sans-serif. A text's width and place differed between platforms, and from the box the core gave it.

Drawings carry Latin, Greek and the symbols of the markup (13.6), and Chinese: the language has Chinese aliases (`rnl-zh`) and the corpus draws Chinese labels. The fonts must cover Simplified Chinese, be free to ship with the desktop app and the web site, and be small enough for a browser to load what a drawing uses.

Facts that bear on the choice:

- Noto Sans CJK, the design Adobe also releases as Source Han Sans (思源黑体), covers Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, symbols and CJK in one design under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Its Simplified Chinese subset, Noto Sans SC, maps 30,890 code points; at the noto-cjk tag Sans2.004 its Regular face is 8.3 MB and its Bold 8.5 MB, in OpenType with CFF outlines.
- Its metrics: 1000 units per em; an ascender of 1.16 em and a descender of 0.288 em, with no line gap; the ideographs' em box from −0.12 to 0.88 em; every ideograph 1 em wide. The advances of the code points it maps, in both faces, fall into 850 runs of equal advances.
- Its layout features include kerning and the ligatures ff, fi, fl, ffi and ffl, which browsers apply by default and which change the advances.
- Split into WOFF2 slices by Unicode range, the two faces are 17.3 MB; a browser loads only the slices whose ranges the text on a page uses.

## Decision

1. **The core ships Noto Sans SC Regular and Bold, chosen for now.** They come from the noto-cjk repository at tag Sans2.004, copyright 2014-2021 Adobe, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 ([`LICENSES/OFL-1.1.txt`](../../LICENSES/OFL-1.1.txt)). Every text is laid out and drawn in this family; the font a style names (9.3) stays a hint that the drawing does not follow.
2. **The fonts are not in the repository; their metrics are.** [`scripts/fonts.py`](../../scripts/README.md) downloads the two files into the ignored `target/fonts/` and checks pinned SHA-256 sums, and writes from them [`crates/rnl-render/fonts/noto-sans-sc.metrics`](../../crates/rnl-render/README.md#fonts), 8.5 KB: the units per em, the ascender, descender and line gap, the top of the em box, the advance of .notdef, and the advance of every code point the fonts map, in runs of equal advances. The file is committed, read with `include_bytes!`, and licensed under OFL-1.1 in `REUSE.toml`; CI writes it again from the pinned fonts and fails when it differs.
3. **The renderer lays out text, and the primitives carry the result.** A text primitive gives its runs, each a piece of text in one face and size, from the start of its baseline in scene units, with its width; and its bars, the bars of fractions and the lines over overlined runs, as strokes. A run is as wide as the sum of its characters' advances, with no kerning, ligatures or other shaping; a character the fonts do not map is 1 em wide, as their .notdef box. Bold runs take the bold advances; italic has no face, so an italic run leans by a quarter of the height, about 14°, with the upright advances. Lines are the fonts' line height apart, 1.448 em, and the vertical alignments are taken from the ascender and descender. Sub- and superscripts are placed by proportions of the renderer's own. Fractions and overlines are placed from the em box: a fraction's bar is at its middle, the numerator and the denominator sit as close to the bar as keeps their em boxes clear of it, and an overline is above it, so that ideographs, which fill the em box, keep clear of their bars as letters do. The view types document these proportions.
4. **A frontend draws the runs in the shipped fonts and lays out nothing.** It draws each run at its point with no kerning or ligatures, turned or leaning as the text and run say, and each bar as a stroke. The SVG writes each run as a `text` element with explicit `x` and `y`; its root names Noto Sans SC first, then the same design under the names it is installed by (Noto Sans CJK SC, Source Han Sans SC), then sans-serif, with `font-kerning: none`, `font-variant-ligatures: none`, `text-spacing-trim: space-all` and `text-autospace: no-autospace`, so that nothing changes the advances; each `text` element keeps its spaces with `xml:space="preserve"`, since Chrome and WebKit collapse the spaces at the ends of a `text` element whatever its ancestors say. Every run has `textLength`, with `lengthAdjust="spacingAndGlyphs"`: in the shipped fonts it is the run's own width and changes nothing visible, but in another face, as when an exported SVG is opened where the fonts are missing or the page runs without its web fonts, it holds each run to the width the core laid out, so that runs that follow each other, centred and right-aligned texts and fractions keep their places and the boxes stay true. It costs about 50 bytes a run.
5. **The viewers' page gets the fonts as web fonts.** `scripts/fonts.py web` writes the two faces as WOFF2 slices by Unicode range into the ignored [`apps/viewer-ui/fonts/`](../../apps/viewer-ui/README.md#fonts), without layout tables: Latin with the symbols of the markup, the other symbols, the CJK punctuation, the 3755 common hanzi of GB 2312 in slices of 50, its 3008 others in slices of 250, and the rest in slices of 1000 code points; with `fonts.css`, their `@font-face` rules, and the licence. The page links `fonts.css` and names only these files, not local fonts, so that the glyphs are those the core measured; `font-display: swap` shows texts at once, and since every run is placed by the core, the swap moves nothing. The desktop app embeds the directory with the page, the web viewer's build copies it, and the workflows that build them write it first. The page works without it.
6. **The fonts' notice goes wherever the fonts or their metrics go** (OFL 2). The metrics are under OFL-1.1, so `rnl-render`, which embeds them, declares `MIT AND OFL-1.1`, the licence of its contents as decision 0002 asks. The web fonts come with `OFL.txt`, the licence with the fonts' copyright; the web site has it in `fonts/` even when it is built without the web fonts, since its core embeds the metrics; and the desktop app's About box gives the fonts' copyright and licence, since the app embeds the web fonts in its executable.

## Consequences

- Texts are placed alike on every platform and in every frontend, up to rasterization, as decision 0006 requires, and a text's bounding box is exact rather than estimated. The corpus's SVGs change only in their texts and in the frames that the texts' boxes change; the models and the conformance corpus's expected files do not change, and native and WebAssembly Documents stay identical.
- Lines are 1.448 times the size apart instead of 1.15, and texts aligned at the top sit lower than before, below the room the fonts keep above capitals.
- The desktop app grows by the web fonts, 17.3 MB, and the web site carries them; a drawing in Latin loads the 45 KB Latin slice, one with a few Chinese labels a few hundred KB. `fonts.css` is 229 KB, 69 KB gzip-compressed, because the common hanzi are listed one by one. The slices follow GB 2312, not frequency of use; slices by frequency would load less for a Chinese page.
- Building the viewers with their fonts needs Python with fontTools and brotli, and downloads 17 MB from GitHub.
- Nothing is shaped: scripts that need shaping, such as Arabic or the Indic scripts, combining marks, and proportional CJK punctuation are drawn as the advances say, and kerning is given up for a layout that is the same everywhere.
- Left to later decisions: other scripts, such as the Japanese and Korean faces of Noto Sans CJK or other Noto families; a monospace face; an italic face; embedding the fonts a drawing uses, subset, in exported SVGs, so that they look the same anywhere; and shaping, if a script needs it. Changing the fonts changes the metrics file and the layout of every text.
