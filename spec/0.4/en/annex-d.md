## Annex D Changes from version 0.3 (informative)

Version 0.4 writes every fact on positions with its direction, so that a fact means one layout and not a choice among several. Versions 0.x make no promise of compatibility with each other (16.1); this annex lists what changed and how a file moves to 0.4. This standard describes version 0.4 only; a file of 0.3 is moved to 0.4 with an upgrade tool that reads version 0.3, which makes the changes at the end of this annex (16.1).

| Change | See |
|---|---|
| Directions are written out. ORDER and DISTANCE take only the four directions; `"x"` and `"y"`, which left the direction open, are wrong parameter values. DISTRIBUTE takes a direction in place of an axis, in the parameter `direction` (no longer `axis`): its members follow one another strictly in that direction, in equal steps, and two members make an ORDER. ALIGN takes one projection, not a list | 14.1–14.5, 8.5 |
| ORIENT and ORIENT-RELATION take a list of codes, such as `["R0", "MY"]`: one fact, which holds when one of them holds; the solver chooses and writes back a single code. Orientation is the only kind of fact that offers such a choice | 11.2, 14.9, 8.5 |
| ORIENT-RELATION relates one object to one object. A set in one slot is done member by member, one fact for each member; a set of more than one member in both slots is an error. Version 0.3 related two sets as wholes | 14.9, 5.2.1 |
| Member places: a reference to a set followed directly by `{…}` takes members by their places, counted over its members spread one level, such as `%a{1}`, `%a{end}`, `%a{2:end}`; a usage takes its members by role, and places of a selection are an error. In 0.3, `%a{#b}` was two objects | 5.2.3, 3.2, 3.3, 3.5, 4.1, 6.10 |
| Constraints take only the lowest objects. A set has no position: no centre and no extent, and its `::position` is the set of the positions of its members. A set keeps its items as written; in the member list of ALIGN, ORDER and DISTRIBUTE, an item that is a set (a `{…}` or a reference to a set) stands for each of its members, one constraint for each combination, each a unit of resolution; DISTANCE relates every member of one slot to every member of the other. In 0.3 a reference in a set was spread, `{%x}` counted by its centre, and DISTANCE compared extents | 14.1, 14.3–14.5, 5.2, 5.2.1, 6.5, 8.5, 11.1, 11.2 |
| A usage is no longer drawn at the point of the object that fills its first role: its symbol is drawn at its own point, and a definition that draws a mark on a pin ties its point to the pin in its body. A pin that binds a role is drawn on the net of what fills it, so `$GROUND @ATTACH #M5:3` attaches a ground to the net of M5:3, at a position of its own; a number in the symbol of a usage binds no role. In 0.3 that usage was drawn on the pin and had no position of its own | 12.1, 12.2, 12.5, 12.6, 6.5, 8.2, 9.2.7, 11.2, 11.3, 13.2, 14.1, 15.2 |
| A general definition may have no roles (`roles=[]`), applied as `$PORT_LIST @ATTACH`. Its body may hold rules: they act as rules of the level of the usage, at the position of its statement, and `#` in the body is that level. A lone `?` in a general definition takes a path, such as `?::position` | 9.4, 8.2, 10.4, 4.1 |
| This standard describes version 0.4 only: files of 0.3 and 0.2 are files of other major versions, moved with an upgrade tool that reads version 0.3, to the language 0.4 and the standard series 0.4. The standard 0.3 described how a tool reads files of 0.2 | 16.1 |
| The standard package is no longer used implicitly: a file writes `@USE-PACKAGE(name="rnl-std-all", …)`, or a tool adds it by a setting, such as `--include-std` or a setting of a viewer, and writes it into the file when it writes the file. In 0.3 it was used at the root of every file | 16.3, 16.1, 16.2, 6.1 |
| Literal coordinates in the body of a general definition, of points and of PLACE, are offsets from the usage's own point, and the ORIENT of the usage does not turn them. In 0.3 they were coordinates of the level | 9.4, 11.1, 11.2, 13.4 |
| DISTANCE states the order as well: its bounds are not negative, and a missing `min` is 0. In 0.3 a DISTANCE with a direction took bounds of either sign | 14.5 |
| Rules and phases: the rules of WIRE are gone, and BUNDLE, WIRE and RECTANGLE, CIRCLE and ELLIPSE written with two points take no result of a selection, nor a set that depends on one; the rules of the four route keywords are evaluated in a step of their own, step 5, before generation, which sees their trees; the rules of LINE, ARC and BEZIER and of CURRENT are evaluated in step 6 with the generation rules; a CURRENT on a pin takes effect once the symbols are given. In 0.3 these rules took effect in the phase of their keyword | 10.4, 8.2, 11.8, 13.1, 9.2.5, 15.2 |
| Every usage has its own point, also one without a symbol, written `%u::position`: PLACE takes it, and so do constraints on a usage drawn with a symbol. In the member list of a constraint, a usage without a symbol stands for its members, down to the lowest objects, as in GROUP, and a combination that takes one object twice is left out. A lone `?` in the body of a general definition is the usage being expanded. A hint is given when a constrained point of a usage is tied to nothing drawn. In 0.3, PLACE and constraints on a usage without a symbol failed | 14.1, 9.4, 12.1, 6.2, 6.5, 3.3, 7.1, 8.2, 11.1 |
| Selective routing: ROUTE is replaced by ROUTE-NET (a whole net, as ROUTE did), ROUTE-OBJECT (the pins of some objects, net by net), UNROUTE-NET and UNROUTE-OBJECT (pins left out). Each pin is decided by the statement of highest priority that covers it, then the later one. Trees on one net that share a pin or are joined by written wires are one tree. A pin in a tree counts as `wired` | 13.7, 10.1, 10.2, Annex A |
| The solver may split a wire written by the author, to branch from its middle, on a net that a tree joins; it rewrites the wire in its record and keeps the author's points in order | 13.1, 13.7 |
| Groups are not objects: no position, no constraints on a group. The members of a group are the objects in its set, down to the lowest; a usage drawn with a symbol is a member itself; ROUTE-OBJECT follows its roles too and also takes its own pins. The tree of groups, and keeping outsiders out of a group's region, belong to the solver as criteria after every fact. GROUP takes `spacing_x` and `spacing_y`, the spacing inside the group in grid units | 11.5, 13.7 |
| SELECT-INSTANCE takes the condition `terminals`, the number of terminals of the instance, so that, for example, MOS transistors with three and with four terminals get different symbols | 10.1, Annex A |
| The Spectre primitive `tline` has the default terminal names `p1`, `n1`, `p2` and `n2`, as T has in SPICE; the table of model families adds masters and the MESFET family | 6.12 |
| An instance that no statement or rule gives a symbol has the default block symbol: a square box centred on its origin, with a pin for each port, or for each node of an instance whose definition is not read; no instance is left undrawn, and a viewer no longer draws a stand-in of its own | 9.2.6, 9.2.8 |
| The record head field `language` makes a record act only on the objects in sections of that netlist language; a file may have a header record for each language besides the shared one. The standard series 0.4 uses it to give the SPICE sections of a Spectre file the rules of SPICE; the series 0.4 does not drop the package of the other dialect implicitly, as the series 0.3 did | 2.8, 2.2, 6.12, 16.3, Annex C |
| The language a file starts in is an input to the tool, by default from its suffix, and the user can change it for each file | 2.2 |
| Rules apply to their own level by default: `recursive` is `false` by default, and a rule with a selection written in it reaches the levels below only with `recursive=true` on one of these selections, and a rule without one reaches as far as the sets it uses; every selection and set of the rule then selects in each level. In 0.3 each selection had its own `recursive`. A rule in a package counts as written in the level of its USE-PACKAGE, and a package at the root in the top level of its file, so the rules of the standard package write `recursive=true`; a generation rule warns about objects of its level only when it reaches the levels below. A rule that reaches the levels below and whose other slots name host objects of its level has no effect in the levels below, without a warning | 10.1, 10.3, 10.4, 10.5, 6.1, 8.3, 16.2, 4.5, Annex B, Principles 2.5 |
| SOLVER-CONFIG keeps the settings of the solver in the file, or in the header record of a package for the levels that use it: named parameters whose keys the solver defines, of which the standard names only `spacing_x`, `spacing_y` and `junction`, the definition of the dots that every solver draws with; later settings override earlier ones key by key; the core passes the settings to the solver as written, in order; a value that is not taken fails only its key, and the value from before stays; budgets of time or work are given only by the tool | 14.10, 14.8, Annex C |
| CURRENT is not a geometric fact, only a hint to the solver; ORDER compares points, and keeping the boxes of symbols apart is a criterion of the solver; ORDER is short for a DISTANCE with `min=1` between neighbours; a renderer without a result draws the first code of an ORIENT list | 15.2, 14.3, 11.2 |
| Clarified: a TEXT with empty content draws nothing; the solver replaces generated records whole, whether or not it understands their statements; the terminals of a bundle correspond by name, and members that differ in their terminal names or model give a warning | 13.3, 2.8, 11.8 |
| Pins are objects, kind `pin`, separate from terminals. A pin is made by PIN in a symbol and named by its local name, `#X1::vdd_t`; `terminal=` may be left out. A terminal may have several pins; where a pin is needed, a terminal stands for its pin, the last one written. Pins no longer replace one another by terminal. A pin name may not be the same as a role name, local name, standard sub-object name or key of a text; `::` looks up pin names before the keys of texts | 9.2.4, 9.2.7, 5.1, 6.4, 6.5, 13.3 |
| SET-SYMBOL matches the terminal names of the symbol to the terminals of the object by name, or by place for numbers; `terminals=` changes the matching, listed in the order of the nodes or as pairs | 11.3 |
| Usages have no terminals: `%g:p` of 0.3 is now a named pin such as `%g::pin`. The pins of a usage bind roles by name and are drawn on what fills them; they are not electrical terminals | 12.1, 6.4 |
| Objects given for another kind are converted only by a table: toward the drawing, terminal to pin to point and drawn object to position; toward the circuit, pin to terminal and pin or terminal to net | 5.2.4 |
| SELECT-PIN selects pins; `wired` belongs to pins and nets; SELECT-TERMINAL selects every terminal, with a pin or not, and steps through terminals count them all. SELECT-INSTANCE and SELECT-USAGE take `symbol` and `drawn`. RECTANGLE, CIRCLE and ELLIPSE written with a size take the result of a selection one by one; the condition `type` takes the wildcard `*`, and `type="*"` selects the instances that have a type | 10.1, 10.2, 8.2, 9.2.5, 13.3 |
| Assertions: ASSERT-INSTANCE, ASSERT-TERMINAL, ASSERT-NET and ASSERT-USAGE check that members meet the conditions of the selection of their kind, ASSERT-USAGE also that they are usages of a definition; ASSERT-IN checks that the members are in a set. They check that attributes are written (`has`) and are the same (`same`) or different (`different`); ASSERT-TOPOLOGY checks that members are connected in series, in parallel, as a star or as a single loop, a usage or a set counting as a black box; a check that does not hold gives a violation, a fourth kind of report besides error, warning and hint, and makes no statement fail. The CURRENT statements of one net together check that the current does not all flow one way; in 0.3 nothing was checked | 15.2, 15.3, 15.4, 7.6, Annex C |
| TABLE lays out objects as a table of nested `{…}`, rows from top to bottom (or with `transpose=true` columns from left to right), each nested list at right angles; one fact, and its objects one group | 14.11 |
| Text: math mode `\(…\)` with a subset of LaTeX math; `\subrest{…}` and `\subdigits{…}` show the rest of a name, or its digits at the end, as a subscript; TEXT takes `fix_rotate`, which keeps the direction of the characters while the side of the text box still follows the orientation of the object | 13.6, 13.3 |
| Aliases end at any character that is not a letter, digit or underscore, in any script; no rule names Chinese punctuation. The Chinese alias package is `alias-zh`. The standard sentences are given in English only | 16.5, 4.5 |
| The word list adds `removed`, the words that 0.4 no longer has; the schema adds `open_params` and `removed`, and `at_least_one` becomes a list of groups, as `together` and `exclusive` are. | 5.3 |
| The empty node, `&` alone, says that there is no net: a role of kind `net` and the net slot of WIRE take it, so that virtual parts can draw a block diagram that has no netlist lines; it is not an object, and two places that write it have nothing in common | 6.3, 9.4, 12.1, 13.1 |
| In a level record, `junction` of SOLVER-CONFIG may name a `%` definition of that level, so that a dot variant defined for one subcircuit stays in it | 13.2, 14.10 |
| `$NO_CONNECT` takes a terminal as itself, so that a mark on one member of a bus says which terminal is left open | 12.6 |
| TEXT: `rotate` takes any angle; `upright` (default `true`) turns a text for reading, and with `false` the characters follow rotation and mirroring, for signs such as arrows; the text style property `textvisible` hides a text, which then takes no room while its content stays | 9.2.5, 9.3, 13.3 |
| ANNOTATION gathers the graphics of a symbol, a general definition or a usage into one annotation, at a point or on an object, turned by any angle; pins and the rest are left out, and so are the graphics of a body tied to other objects | 13.5 |
| An implementation may set limits on resources, such as the depth of nesting or the number of objects; input beyond a limit gives an error of the implementation, never a result cut short in silence; at least 64 levels of nesting of symbols are supported | 1.6 |
| An implementation sets a limit on the time or the work it spends on checking facts; this standard does not fix how the limit is measured | 8.5 |
| DEVICE takes `type`, which gives an instance that the host gives no type, such as a PDK device, the type that selections and assertions read; `name` or `model`, and `terminals` or `type`, may each be left out but not both, and for the names and the type separately the last matching DEVICE that writes them counts. The default terminal names of the Spectre `transformer` are `p1 n1 p2 n2` | 6.12 |
| The result of a selection is in a defined order: the order of the scene, or scope member by scope member without repeats, the objects made by rules after those written | 10.3 |
| Pins face a direction in the drawing, which ORIENT and ORIENT-RELATION written on pins constrain; ORIENT takes the words `left`, `down`, `right` and `up` for R0, R90, R180 and R270 | 9.2.4, 11.2, 14.9 |
| A module of Verilog-A brought in with `ahdl_include` or `.hdl` can give its instances a block symbol, written in `//` records inside the module; the module is a level with no circuit that holds only symbols and styles, and a vector port is one terminal for each bit, in the order of its declared range | 2.9, 2.2, 6.1, 9.2.6, 17 |

**Moving a file from 0.3 to 0.4.** This standard does not describe files of version 0.3; an upgrade tool that reads version 0.3 moves them (16.1). It raises the version in the header record, moves the file to the standard series 0.4, and makes the changes below; what it cannot change, it reports for the author:

- `"x"` or `"y"` as the direction of ORDER or DISTANCE, and `axis=` for DISTRIBUTE: choose a direction.
- The terminal names `p_plus`, `p_minus`, `s_plus` and `s_minus` of a Spectre `transformer`: write `p1`, `n1`, `p2` and `n2`.
- Sets on both sides of ORIENT-RELATION: write one fact for each pair.
- Rules without `recursive`: a tool writes `recursive=true` in their selections, in the file, its own package files and its inline packages, so that they keep reaching the levels below. A rule that writes `recursive=false` on some of its selections and not on others is reported: in 0.4, one `true` makes the whole rule reach below.
- ROUTE: a tool writes ROUTE-NET in its place.
- WIRE or BUNDLE given the result of a selection: write ROUTE-OBJECT for the wires, and the members of a bundle one by one.
- `%g:p` and other terminals of usages: write the named pin of the symbol, such as `%g::pin`. The symbols of the series 0.3 bind the roles of usages by number, which 0.4 does not do (11.3).
- `$GROUND @ATTACH #M5:3` and other virtual parts whose first role is a terminal or a pin: they now stand on that net at a position of their own; to keep them on the pin, write a stub, `$GROUND_STUB @ATTACH #M5:3`, or constraints that put the pin of the ground on the pin of M5 (12.3).
- `wired=` on SELECT-TERMINAL: write SELECT-PIN, such as `# @SELECT-INSTANCE => @SELECT-PIN(wired=false)`.
- `{%x}` counted by its centre, and `::position` of a set, which was its centre: a set has no position in 0.4, and its `::position` is the set of the positions of its members; the upgrade leaves such a statement for review. To place the objects as one, make them the members of a usage whose body ties them to its own point, and constrain `%u::position` (14.1).
- A usage in the member list of a constraint, which counted by one point: the upgrade writes `%u::position` for a usage without a symbol, which would otherwise stand for its members (14.1).
- DISTANCE between two sets: it now holds for every pair, so a `max` bounds every pair and not the gap between extents.
- Literal coordinates in the body of a general definition: they are offsets from the usage's own point; a definition that meant the coordinates of the level has its usages placed at `(0, 0)`, such as `$TITLE @ATTACH => @PLACE(0, 0)`.
- The standard package: write `@USE-PACKAGE(name="rnl-std-all", version="0.4")`, and rewrite the uses of the series 0.3 as "The standard series 0.4" below says: the new names and roles, a port tag `$IO_PORT @ATTACH` for each port net that the fill of 0.3 tagged, and a report for each use of a definition that was removed.

**The standard series 0.4.** The standard package is not part of this standard (16.3); what follows is what matters to a file that moves from the series 0.3 to the series 0.4.

- **Using it**: a file writes `@USE-PACKAGE(name="rnl-std-all", version="0.4")` in its header record, or is read with a setting of the tool that adds it (16.3).
- **Ports**: the fill gives every port net a port tag, supply and ground nets too, in one column at least 6 units left of the level's instances, each tag at R0; a file takes nets out of `$PORT_FILL_SET`, writes `$IO_PORT @ATTACH` for a tag of its own, or `$PORT_LIST @ATTACH` for the column written out (12.4).
- **The fill**: it adds net labels, each a short stub, to pins without wires; no net is drawn as wires unless a file or a package says so with ROUTE-NET or ROUTE-OBJECT; the ground and supply nets are left out with `@UNROUTE-NET(priority="high")`, and a file that wants them drawn writes `@ROUTE-NET(priority="high")` or WIREs. The fill adds no ground or supply symbol to pins that have wires, whether the file wires them by hand or draws them with ROUTE-NET or ROUTE-OBJECT, written as statements or as rules; a file that wants one attaches it, such as `$GROUND @ATTACH &vss` for a net drawn whole or `$GROUND_STUB @ATTACH #R1:2` on one pin. Its rules write `recursive=true` and select pins, those of virtual parts included. The nets in `$BIAS_NET_SET` get bias marks as the ground and supply nets get theirs.
- **The packages**: the one collection is `rnl-std-all`, which references every package of the series and has a header record for each language (2.8, 16.3); the collections `rnl-std`, `rnl-std-core` and `rnl-std-full` of the series 0.3 are gone, and `rnl-std-structures` is `rnl-std-semantics`. `rnl-std-no-labels` selects with `drawn=true` and hides labels with the style `textvisible=false` instead of empty texts, so a file shows one again with `textvisible=true`. The optional `rnl-std-45deg` is new.
- **Renamed or changed**: `$DIFFERENTIAL_PAIR` becomes `$DIFFERENTIAL_PAIR_N` and `$DIFFERENTIAL_PAIR_P`, with the roles `left`, `right` and `tail`; `$MIRRORED` is renamed `$MIRRORED_LAYOUT`, with the roles `left`, `right` and one `axis`, and the new `$MIRRORED_VERTICAL_LAYOUT` has `top`, `bottom` and `axis`, since a definition that only places ends in `_LAYOUT`; the `_N` and `_P` structures assert the polarity of their transistors; `$FLOW` becomes `$SIGNAL_FLOW`, which places nothing, takes no direction and may name the sources and the loads of its path, and `$POWER_FLOW` and `$BIAS_CIRCUIT` are new; `$CURRENT_MIRROR` becomes `$CURRENT_MIRROR_N` and `$CURRENT_MIRROR_P`; the role of `$NO_CONNECT` takes a terminal or a pin. The complete list of the definitions that the series renames, merges, splits or removes is in the description of the standard package; the command `rnl upgrade` in the last release of the RNL core 0.5.x (a version of the tool, not of this language) rewrites files by it. Every fact of position in the structures states its direction, and the other drawing is a variant of its own, such as `$BRANCH_HORIZONTAL`.
- **Removed**: `$CHAIN`, `$SUPPLY_HIGH` and `$SUPPLY_LOW`.
